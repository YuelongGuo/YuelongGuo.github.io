01

Computational pathology

Histology imaging & molecular subtyping

Joining histology imaging with molecular subtyping: using routine stained slides to assign cancer patients to subtypes ordinarily defined by expression profiling. The premise is that the transcriptional programs separating those subtypes leave signatures in tissue architecture and nuclear morphology, so a slide already cut for diagnosis can carry information otherwise bought with a separate molecular assay. The difficulty is generalization — a subtype label belongs to the patient while morphology varies from field to field across a whole slide, and stain and scanner variation between sites is easily mistaken for biological signal.

02 — Volunteer education

Designing the path, then walking it.

Every program below was built as well as taught: scope, sequence, and materials first, then the classroom. The common thread is mechanism — students should leave able to explain what the tool is doing, not just operate it.

Ongoing program

AI for Youth Academy

“Build world-changing ideas with AI, one year at a time.”

A five-year, project-based pathway through AI, mathematics, programming, and machine learning for middle and high school students — summer camps, weekend studios, and after-school labs.

↗

Courses designed and taught

  1. 2023–2024

    Introduction to AI

    Cary Chinese School

    A first course in AI built for intuition rather than recipes — what each algorithm is actually doing under the hood, and why it works.

    • Open at linear regression: loss, fitting, and gradient descent worked through by hand
    • Build up to neural networks one component at a time, so nothing arrives as magic
    • Explore the range of deep learning architectures and what each one is shaped for
    • Keep the emphasis on mechanism throughout — students should be able to explain the model, not just run it

04 — About

Find the mechanism. Hand it to someone else.

One question, three settings

I have spent my career asking the same question of very different systems: what is the mechanism underneath? Plant and nematode genomes gave way to human population genetics, transfusion medicine to neurodegeneration — but the method never changed. Define the measurement carefully, respect what the data can and cannot support, and leave behind an analysis someone else can reproduce.

AI changed the pace of that work rather than its nature. It lets me search a proteome or a stack of images for structure I would never have found by hand, and it collapses the distance between having an idea and testing it. What it does not do is explain itself — which is exactly why I teach it the way I do.

In the classroom I would rather a student understand one algorithm from the inside than run ten of them as black boxes. My courses open at linear regression and build upward, because the intuition transfers even as the tools keep changing. The aim is always the same: give someone enough understanding to go and build the thing they were already imagining.

The volunteer work comes from the same place. A community, like a model, only works when enough people are willing to look under the hood and explain what they find.
